## Purpose and promise

Build a memorable, welcoming first encounter with ZAM for children in Grade
4 and their parents. Start with mathematics in Bavaria, short explanations
by real teachers, interactive practice and later recall. Let a colourful
world reflect the child's growing understanding.

The parent promise is: **See what your child understands, help with the next
small step, and make mathematics easier to practise together.** The child
promise is: **You and your companion discover what mathematics can do.**

The transition to secondary school makes this a timely entry point. It
does not justify promising a Gymnasium place or treating another school
path as failure. Mathematics is only one part of the transition: the
ordinary direct-entry Gymnasium route considers German, mathematics and
Heimat- und Sachunterricht together. See the
[Bavarian ministry's transition guidance](https://www.km.bayern.de/lernen/ubertritt-und-bildungswege/uebertritt).

## Recommended experience

Choose a **discovery island with a companion**, rather than a separate pet
game beside a worksheet. The companion, provisionally Milo, is an original
small creature with a recognisable silhouette, expressive face and a home
the child helps develop. Two presentation directions are worth testing:

- **Discovery island:** the mathematical landscape leads; the companion
  travels with the learner. This is the recommended lighthouse direction.
- **Companion garden:** the creature and its home lead; a short mission
  list offers a quieter, more intimate alternative.

The visual style uses saturated accents, warm illustrations, clear type,
large touch targets and restrained animation. Colour belongs to the world;
the mathematical workspace stays calm. Short musical phrases mark a real
milestone. Sound starts off, is enabled by a deliberate action and remains
optional. Every spoken instruction also has text; reduced motion and a
quiet presentation remain available.

### Companion rules

Keep the care and attachment of the proposed Tamagotchi, but change the
consequence of absence. A creature that becomes weak because a child has
not studied could turn a difficult subject into another obligation. This
is a design risk to test with families, not a claim of demonstrated harm.

- A completed, meaningful practice step earns a small provision for Milo:
  a snack, a plant or something for the next expedition.
- A wrong answer does not remove food, health or affection. Using a hint,
  trying a strategy and working through a correction can complete the step.
- Absence means rest. Milo naps or explores at home and welcomes the child
  back. There is no death, illness, lost decoration, broken streak or debt.
- Care itself remains available without earning a currency. Rewards add
  variety; they do not determine whether the creature is safe.
- Reward accepted practice events, with a small session allowance. Neither
  elapsed screen time, replayed videos nor repeated easy taps earns more.
- Learning and prerequisite decisions stay independent of rewards. A snack
  is not evidence of mastery, and failure never traps the child in a
  compulsory practice loop.
- End sessions clearly. Free exploration and the companion can be disabled.
  There are no competitive rankings, purchases or chance-based rewards.

At the move to a later grade, the companion can settle into an archived
home. The learner keeps their learning history and can choose the calmer
ZAM presentation. This experiment need not redefine later-grade products.

## Curriculum and learning design

The official [LehrplanPLUS mathematics curriculum](https://www.lehrplanplus.bayern.de/fachlehrplan/grundschule/4/mathematik)
combines Grades 3 and 4. Grade 4 therefore needs a teacher-reviewed
progression, with earlier foundations available when needed; the official
document alone does not assign every competence to Grade 4.

| World | Curriculum area | Example interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number Forest | Numbers and operations | Bundle place values, explain regrouping, check a calculation |
| Shape Bay | Space and shape | Complete a reflection, build with cubes, describe a route |
| Measure Market | Quantities and measurement | Measure, compare units, plan a purchase or journey |
| Chance Garden | Data and chance | Collect observations, read a chart, compare possible outcomes |

Word problems, reasoning, communicating and representations run through
the worlds. Paper work and physical measuring remain part of practice.

Use a short, optional entry check to offer an appropriate starting point.
Offer help with a prerequisite at the point of need, without displaying a
lower-grade label or requiring an exhaustive test before the first lesson.

A proposed session lasts roughly 8–12 minutes, adjusted after observation:

1. Retrieve a small amount of previously learned material.
2. Meet one concrete question, with a short teacher explanation if needed.
3. Manipulate a representation and solve a few varied tasks.
4. Explain or check a strategy, including on paper where appropriate.
5. Celebrate one concrete achievement, care for Milo and finish.

The first demonstration should use **place value and regrouping**. For
example, exchanging one hundred for ten tens visibly supplies a bridge;
then the child solves a different example without the same support. The
change in representation is the spectacle. An incorrect answer prompts a
specific hint while Milo stays supportive.

Self-rating alone cannot establish arithmetic competence. Combine checked
responses, observed steps and short explanations. Repeated recall supports
retention; it does not replace learning written procedures or transfer.

## Teacher videos are part of the lesson

Use a qualified primary-school teacher as a visible, recurring guide.
An initial target is 1–3 minutes per explanation, split where necessary.
Show hands, manipulatives and worked steps as well as the person. The
teacher and interactive activity must use compatible terminology and
methods; review written subtraction and division particularly carefully.

For the demonstration, commission or record one complete original clip
with a teacher and documented permission. Include captions and a readable
worked explanation. A storyboard or temporary video link must be labelled
as such; a funding demonstration must distinguish finished material from
planned production.

For the wider course, curate existing teacher videos first where they fit,
then produce missing explanations. Maintain a register of competence,
author, exact URL, relevant section, age suitability, method, captions,
availability and permission to embed or distribute. Public availability
does not establish permission to copy a video. Prefer cleared hosting for
the eventual child experience; external links are an explicit fallback.
The written lesson and practice remain usable when a video is unavailable.
Do not imply that teacher participation or video rights are already secured.

## What makes parents say “wow”

The spectacle should lead immediately to an understandable learning result.
Use a separate parent view with a few plain-language observations:

- what the child solved independently, and when;
- what still needed a representation or hint;
- the next suggested activity and a short way to help at home.

Use “practised”, “with help” and “recalled later”, supported by dated
examples. Do not infer a school grade, a diagnostic label or school-path
suitability from a few app tasks. Clearly distinguish curriculum coverage,
lesson completion and evidence of retained knowledge.

## Demonstration and funding case

Make one complete, polished learning journey before filling the annual
course with shallow content. A three-minute demonstration can show:

1. The child meets Milo and chooses a mission.
2. A real teacher introduces regrouping.
3. The child exchanges place values and applies the idea.
4. A mistake receives useful support and a second attempt.
5. Milo's world gains a small feature; the parent sees the specific skill.
6. A clearly labelled later-session example shows retrieval of the idea.

The lighthouse claim is a proposition to investigate: accessible,
curriculum-aligned practice that children willingly revisit and parents
can understand. The distinctive combination is teacher explanation,
interactive mathematics, spaced recall and a companion that welcomes
returning learners.

Recruit a primary-school teacher for content review and a pilot partner
before expanding production. A proposed 6–8-week feasibility pilot with
roughly 30–50 families can examine onboarding, independent use, voluntary
return, parent confidence and unwanted pressure around the companion.
These numbers are planning assumptions, not a powered efficacy study.

Measure learning with separate, equivalent tasks before and after practice
and delayed tasks, for example after one and three weeks. Watching a video
or feeding Milo does not count as learning. Report dropouts, adult help and
device/access barriers alongside positive results. A later comparison of
the same lessons with and without the companion can test its added value;
a simple before/after pilot cannot establish that effect.

Build the funding package from the demonstrated journey, the teacher's
review, the official curriculum coverage matrix, pilot evidence and a
costed production plan. Budget separately for teaching and review, video
production and rights, illustration/animation/sound, engineering,
accessibility and evaluation. No funding programme, eligibility, award or
delivery date is assumed in this proposal.

## Fit with the existing repositories

This document proposes future work; it does not change runtime behaviour.

- The training app and its sole learning cell belong together in
  `zam-os/zam-academy`, provisionally `mathematik-grundschule-4/` and its
  `cell.json`. The final curriculum bindings need a reviewed coverage matrix.
- Follow [the Academy app/cell contract](../README.md#how-an-app-and-its-zam-cell-fit-together):
  stable lesson links, released item identity, joint validation and release
  synchronisation into ZAM. Do not hand-edit the bundled copies in `zam`.
- Existing mathematics apps already provide lessons, manipulatives,
  checked tasks and an optional `TeacherVideo` reference. They are useful
  starting points, not proof that Grade 4 content or the new experience exists.
- Keep the companion a presentation feature. Use accepted learning events
  rather than changing FSRS ratings or inventing a second mastery system.
- The static Academy and ZAM's personal library currently hold different
  kinds of progress. A unified parent view and rewards across both need an
  explicit integration design. Do not present browser progress as ZAM review
  evidence. A pure static app cannot silently read a local ZAM database.
- The demonstration can use local browser state, clearly labelled sample
  parent data and no accounts. Parent access across devices, durable backup,
  synchronisation and research exports require separate design work.
- Keep enrolment possible through the learner UI. The colourful first run
  must not require a terminal, external account or agent configuration.
